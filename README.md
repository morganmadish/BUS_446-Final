# BUS_446-Final
 Final Project on Clean Energy Jobs 

This project analyzes how clean energy development relates to economic performance in the United States, using North Carolina and a ten-state comparison as key examples. It examines trends in clean power capacity, investment, job creation, and economic indicators to assess whether growth in renewable energy aligns with stronger economic outcomes.North Carolina illustrates this transition clearly: rapid solar expansion, major declines in coal use, over $15 billion in clean energy investment, and a steady rise in clean energy jobs—all occurring alongside GDP growth and falling unemployment. Clean energy employment now far exceeds fossil fuel jobs, reflecting a major shift in the state’s energy workforce.A broader comparison of high- and low-investment states shows similar patterns. States that invest heavily in clean power tend to have larger workforces, higher employment, and lower unemployment than states with minimal investment. While influenced by factors such as population and industry mix, the overall findings suggest that clean energy growth is strongly associated with positive economic performance and long-term labor market strength.
